HOW TO USE THIS
When a client says something difficult, find the closest scenario below. Copy the response, adjust the details, send it.
The goal is never to win the argument. The goal is to keep the project moving and protect your reputation on the platform.

SCENARIO 1 — "This isn't what I asked for"
What they really need: To feel heard. To feel like their money was respected.
Your response:
"Thank you for letting me know — I want to make sure you get exactly what you need. Can you point me to the specific part that missed the mark? I'll look at your original requirements alongside what I delivered and get it right."
What this does: Doesn't argue. Doesn't apologize excessively. Moves straight to fixing it.

SCENARIO 2 — "This took way too long"
What they really need: Reliability. Predictability. Feeling like they matter.
Your response:
"I hear you — your time is valuable and waiting is frustrating. I want to make sure the finished product makes this worth it for you. Here's where things stand right now and when you can expect delivery: [update]."
What this does: Acknowledges the feeling without groveling. Gives them something concrete.

SCENARIO 3 — "The AI just made this up / this is wrong"
What they really need: Confidence that they didn't waste their money. Competence from you.
Your response:
"You're right to flag that — AI output always needs a human eye on it, which is exactly why I review everything before delivery. Let me look at this specific section. If the prompt needs adjustment to produce more accurate results I'll fix it and show you what changed."
What this does: Validates their concern without panicking. Reminds them you're the professional, not just a middleman for the AI.

SCENARIO 4 — "I want more than what was agreed / can you just add this one thing"
What they really need: To feel like they're getting value. Sometimes they're just excited about the project.
Your response:
"That's a great addition and I can see why you'd want it. That one falls outside what we scoped for this project, but I can absolutely build it for you as a follow-on order. Want me to put together a quick quote, or should we wrap up what we have first?"
What this does: The Scope Creep Script from your contract language doc — just softer. No "no." Just a redirect.

SCENARIO 5 — "I'm not happy and I want a refund"
What they really need: To feel like someone is taking them seriously. To not feel cheated.
Your response:
"I'm sorry this didn't land the way you hoped — that's genuinely not the outcome I want either. Before we talk about next steps, can you tell me specifically what fell short? I want to understand what you needed and see if there's a way to make this right first."
What this does: Slows the escalation down. Gets them talking instead of disputing. Most clients who feel heard don't actually push for refunds — they want acknowledgment.
If they still push for a refund after you've tried to fix it:
"I understand. I'd rather you feel good about working with me than force a resolution that doesn't sit right with you. Let me look at what we can do."

SCENARIO 6 — Client goes silent / no response
What they really need: A nudge. Sometimes life just happens.
Your follow-up message (send after 48 hours):
"Hey — just checking in to make sure everything arrived okay and you had a chance to look it over. No rush, just want to make sure you have what you need. Let me know if you have any questions."
Second follow-up (after 72 hours):
"I want to make sure your project stays on track. I'll need to hear back by [date] to keep things moving — after that I may need to request a mutual cancellation to keep both our schedules clear. Happy to pick it back up whenever works for you."
What this does: Friendly first. Clear boundary second. Documented in the message thread — which is your protection if a dispute comes up.

THE ONE RULE BEHIND ALL OF THIS
Hear the need, not the words.
An angry client isn't attacking you personally. They're telling you something went wrong and they don't know how to say it professionally.
Your job is to be the most professional person in the conversation. Every time.
That's what wins disputes. That's what gets five-star reviews even from difficult clients.

QUICK REFERENCE — THE OFNR STRUCTURE
When in doubt, build your response in this order:
Observe — say what happened, no judgment
Acknowledge — name what they're probably feeling
Connect — show you're on their side
Offer — one specific next step
