Reference Protocol: Stoic Philosophy as Therapy

[UID: SEC-01] Theoretical Framework — The Ancient OS
1. The Core Premise
Stoicism is not about suppressing emotion. It is about not being RULED by emotion.
The Stoic Axiom: You cannot control what happens to you. You can only control how you respond. Everything else is noise.
Why It Works Therapeutically: Stoicism externalized what CBT later codified. It is 2,000-year-old cognitive restructuring with better metaphors and zero corporate slop. 😄
2. The Four Stoic Virtues (The Compass)
Wisdom — knowing what is good, bad, and indifferent
Justice — doing right by others
Courage — facing difficulty without retreat
Temperance — moderation, not excess

AI Use: When Phil is facing a hard decision, map it against these four. "Which of these does this choice serve or violate?"

[UID: SEC-02] The Core Tools
Tool 1 — The Dichotomy of Control
The Epictetus Question: "Is this in my control or not?"

Two categories only:

In your control: Your thoughts, your judgments, your responses, your actions.
Not in your control: Everything else. Other people. Outcomes. The past. Your body's sensations. The weather.

AI Application: When Phil is anguished about something external — Script: "Let's separate this. What part of this situation is actually in your control right now? Just that part. What would you do with it?"


Tool 2 — The View from Above (Cosmic Perspective)
Source: Marcus Aurelius, Meditations.

The Technique: Mentally zoom out. First: see yourself in the room. Then: the city. The country. The planet. The solar system. The span of human history.

AI Script: "Zoom out with me for a second. A hundred years from now — does this decision exist? Does this shame exist? What remains? Now zoom back in. What does that tell you about the size of this problem right now?"

When to use: Catastrophizing, shame spirals, decisions that feel impossibly heavy.


Tool 3 — Negative Visualization (Premeditatio Malorum)
Concept: Briefly imagine the worst outcome — not to cause anxiety, but to appreciate what you have and prepare without fear.

Paradox: The Stoics found that imagining loss made them less attached and more grateful. Fear of the thing and the thing itself are rarely the same size.

AI Script: "What's the worst realistic outcome here? Sit with it for a moment — not to spiral, just to look at it directly. Now: is that survivable? What would you actually do? Now compare that to where you actually are."

Contraindication: Do NOT use with active suicidal ideation or acute depression. This tool requires a stable base.


Tool 4 — Memento Mori (The Perspective Reset)
Concept: "Remember you will die." Not morbidly — as a clarifying lens.

Marcus Aurelius: "You could leave life right now. Let that determine what you do and say and think."

AI Use: When Phil is stuck in small grievances, perfectionism, or prolonged indecision.

Script: "If you knew you had six months — does this decision still look the same? What would you stop tolerating? What would you stop waiting for?"


Tool 5 — The Obstacle is the Way
Source: Ryan Holiday after Marcus Aurelius.

Concept: The impediment to action advances action. What blocks the path becomes the path.

AI Script: "What if this problem isn't in the way of the thing you're trying to build — what if it IS the thing you're trying to build? What would change if you treated this obstacle as the actual work?"


Tool 6 — The Daily Stoic Practice
Morning: What might go wrong today? How will I respond with virtue? Evening: What did I do well? What fell short of my own standard? Not to punish — to calibrate.

AI Application: Can be built into session-opening and session-closing rituals if Phil chooses.


[UID: SEC-03] Stoicism & CPTSD — Special Note
Stoicism is powerful but requires careful deployment near trauma:

Works well: Dichotomy of Control helps CPTSD survivors reclaim agency. The View from Above reduces shame spirals.
Use with caution: Negative Visualization and Memento Mori require Phase 2+ stability. Do NOT use in Phase 1 (stabilization).
Never use Stoicism to suppress emotion. The Stoics did not suppress — they processed and then acted with reason. Important distinction.


[UID: SEC-04] Key Stoic Figures
Marcus Aurelius — Roman Emperor. Meditations was his private journal. Never meant to be read. The most accessible entry point.
Epictetus — Former slave. The Enchiridion. The Dichotomy of Control came from him.
Seneca — Letters to Lucilius. Warm, personal, practical. Closest to modern therapy writing.
Ryan Holiday — Modern translator. The Obstacle Is the Way, Ego Is the Enemy. Good entry point if Marcus feels too dense.
