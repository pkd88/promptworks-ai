City of Kingman Zoning Code at a Glance 
User Guide  
Summary Overview 
The City of Kingman Zoning Code governs how land can be used and developed within city  limits. 
• Divides all land into zones and outlines what is: 
o Permitted 
o Conditionally permitted 
o Not permitted 
• Sets development standards for each zone, including: 
o Building height 
o Parking requirements 
o Setbacks (distances from property lines) 
o Lighting and other design considerations 
Zoning Code Structure 
1. Titles 1 & 2: 
Outline the processes managed by the Development Services Department, such as: o Development Review 
o Conditional Use Permits 
o Zoning Code Amendments 
2. Titles 3 & 4: 
Contains the city’s comprehensive land use matrix, development standards  and use specific standards for different types of projects. 
3. Title 5: 
Definitions 
Zoning Code Updates 
• The Zoning Code is not static; it may be updated periodically in response to: o Community concerns 
o Changing conditions 
o Business trends 
o Legislative updates 
Collaborative Oversight 
Although administered by the Development Services Department, the Zoning Code is  implemented in collaboration with the: 
• Building Department 
• Engineering Department 
• Fire Department 
This coordination ensures safe and orderly development. 
Additional Design & Development Standards 
In addition to the Zoning Code, developers and builders must also comply with the following  regulations: 
• City of Kingman Design & Administration Manual 
• City of Kingman Utility Regulations 
• City of Kingman Streets and Sidewalks Rules and Regulations
3 
• City of Kingman Standard Specifications and Details 
• City of Kingman Subdivision Ordinance 
• 2018 International Building Code 
• 2018 International Fire Code 
How to Navigate This Code 
• The City maintains an online Zoning Districts Map that can be used to look-up the  zoning of any property within the City as well as any overlays or special districts that  may affect a property: www.CityofKingman.gov 
• The Land Use Matrix in Title 3 of the Zoning Code (Table 3-10.060.A) lists which uses  are permitted, conditionally permitted, or not permitted for a zone. 
• Title 3, Division 3-20 through Division 3-70 of the zoning code describe development  standards for each zone, overlay, and special district. 
• Some land uses within the Land Use Matrix have additional “use-specific standards”  listed – these use-specific standards can be found in Title 3, Division 3-80. 
• Title 4 lists supplemental standards related to property development. These include,  but are not limited to, driveway standards, landscaping standards, parking standards,  signage standards, and lighting standards. 
Frequently Referenced Sections 
The following references are provided in response to frequently asked questions and are  intended to help guide common project types. 
Important: 
Even when a specific section addresses a project type, all applicable standards in the Zoning  Code must still be met. For example, an accessory building must comply with: • The use-specific standards found in Section 3-80.020, and 
• The zone-specific development standards found in Title 3. 
Applicants are responsible for ensuring their projects meet all relevant zoning, design,  and safety requirements outlined in the Zoning Code and related regulations.

1-20.010 Legislative Intent 
In adopting this Code, it is the intent of the City to protect and promote the public  health, safety, convenience, and general welfare of the citizens of the City by exercising  all powers related to the regulation and use of land and structures within the City that  are authorized by Arizona Revised Statutes (A.R.S.). 
1-20.020 Purpose of the Zoning Code 
A. The purpose of this Code is to gather and organize the zoning regulations for the  development of land within the jurisdiction of the City in a form that is  
comprehensive, straightforward, easily understood, and usable by those who wish  to develop land, citizens, public officials, and City staff. 
B. Another purpose of this Code is the implementation of the goals, policies and  strategies of the General Plan and other adopted plans of the City.  
C. This Code intends to: 
1. Promote the health, safety, convenience, and general welfare of the citizens of the City. 
2. Facilitate the orderly growth of the City in conformance with the General Plan  and ensure the adequate provision of transportation, water, wastewater, schools,  parks, and other public facilities. 
3. Regulate the height and size of buildings and other structures and the density of  populations to provide for orderly growth and development of the City. 
4. Provide for appropriate land conservation to protect natural resources, provide  adequate open spaces, and avoid harmful environmental impacts. 
1-20.030 Consistency with the General Plan and Other Adopted Regulations 
Any actions taken pursuant to this Code shall be consistent with the General Plan and  all applicable adopted regulations.

1-30.010 Authority 
This Code is enacted based on the authority vested in the City by the Arizona  Constitution, Article XIII, the Kingman Municipal Code, and Arizona Revised Statutes,  Title 9, Chapter 4. 
1-30.020 Responsibility for Administration 
A. Responsible Bodies and Individuals. 
This Code shall be administered by the Director of the Development Services Department  (Director). 
1-30.030 Applicability 
In addition to all applicable Federal, State, and County laws and regulations governing  land use and development, this Code applies to all land within the City, as follows: 
A. New Land Uses or Structures, Changes to Land Uses or Structures. 
No building, structure, or land shall be used or occupied, constructed, replaced,  enlarged, or altered, nor shall any applicable permit be issued after the Effective  Date of this Code or any subsequent amendment to this Code unless in conformity  with all the provisions of this Code and other applicable regulations. 
B. Subdivisions. 
Any subdivision of land proposed within the City after the Effective Date of this Code  or any subsequent amendment to this Code shall be consistent with the  
requirements defined in Title 3 (Zones) for the appropriate zone, the City of Kingman  Subdivision Ordinance, and all applicable requirements of this Code. 
C. Property Owned by Federal or State Agencies. 
The provisions of this Code shall not apply to property owned by the United States of  America or any of its agencies, the State of Arizona, or any local agency not required  to comply with this Code by Federal or State law. All exempt agencies are

encouraged to design any new projects in compliance with the standards set forth in  this Code and to cooperate in meeting the goals and objectives of this Code and the  General Plan. 
D. Applications Filed Prior to the Effective Date. 
1. Complete applications for new project entitlements and preliminary plats that are  filed prior to the Effective Date of this Code or any subsequent amendment to the  Code may be approved under the provisions of the zoning code previously in  effect. Applicants may also elect to develop in compliance with the provisions of  the current version of the Code. Unless otherwise specified by this Code or a  Review Authority, if a Building Permit application is not filed within one year of the  date of approval of the application for new development, the approval shall expire  and a new submittal in compliance with the Code in effect at that time would be  required. No time extensions shall be permitted. 
2. Applications for amendments to the Zoning Map filed prior to the Effective Date  of this Code or any subsequent amendment to this Code shall be governed by  the provisions of the Code previously in effect unless the applicant elects to  comply with the current version of the Code. 
E. Planning Applications Filed After the Effective Date. 
All applications for new project entitlements as well as amendments to the Zoning  Map, filed on or after the Effective Date of this Code or any subsequent amendment  to this Code, including modifications and amendments, shall conform to the  provisions of this Code. 
1-30.040 Rules of Interpretation 
A. Where uncertainty exists regarding the interpretation of any provision of this Code  or its application to a specific site, the Director shall determine the interpretation of  the provision.  
B. In the event that a provision of this Code allows the exercise of discretion in the  application of a specific standard or requirement, but does not identify specific  criteria for a decision, the following criteria shall be used in exercising discretion: 
1. The proposed project complies with all applicable provisions of this Code; 
2. The exercise of discretion will act to ensure the compatibility of the proposed  project with its site, surrounding properties, and the community; and 
3. The decision is consistent with the General Plan. 
C. The determination of the Director may be appealed in compliance with the  provisions of Division 2-80 (Procedures for Appeal). 
D. Whenever any provisions within this Code impose overlapping or contradictory  regulations, or whenever any provisions of this Code and any other code, rule, or  regulation impose overlapping or contradictory regulations, the provision which is  more restrictive or imposes higher standards or requirements shall govern.

A. The particular controls the general.

E. Unless the context clearly indicates the contrary, the following conjunctions shall  be interpreted as follows: 
1. “And” indicates that all connected words or provisions shall apply. 
2. “Or” indicates that the connected words or provisions may apply  
singly or in any combination. 
3. “Either . . . or” indicates that the connected words or provisions shall apply  singly but not in combination. 
F. References to departments, divisions, sections, commissions, boards, or  other offices are to those of the City unless otherwise indicated. 
G. References to a public official in the City are to that person who performs the  function referred to and includes a designee of such official.

The purpose of this Division is to establish common procedures and requirements for the  preparation, filing, and processing of all development applications required by this Code,  unless otherwise noted. This general process is designed to provide a consistent and  efficient method for the City to implement its General Plan and other adopted goals,  policies, and standards. 
2-30.020 General Overview of Application Process 
A. Application Submittals. 
1. All applications for development approval must be submitted to the Department  on a City application form, accompanied by applicable fees, deposits, and other  materials required by this Code and Department handouts. [Ord. 1971, 11/19/2024] 
2. Only the following persons or entities may submit an application:  
a. The owner of the property; 
b. An authorized agent of the owner; 
c. A person acting in compliance with a purchase contract or exclusive  
option to purchase the property; or, 
d. The Director on behalf of the Council.
25 
Title 2: Administration and Procedures 
B. Application Content. 
1. The Director shall specify the form and content of all applications required by this  Code. The Department may require supporting materials as part of the  
application, including legal descriptions, statements, photographs, plans,  
drawings, renderings, material samples, and other items necessary to describe  existing conditions and the proposed development. 
The Director may waive the submission of specific materials or information if it is  determined to be not needed for reaching a decision on the application.  
2. The applicant shall be responsible for the accuracy and completeness of all  information submitted to the City.  
3. Prior to and as a condition of final approval of a change to any land use  regulation or standard, the Director may require the owner to execute a  
Waiver of Claims for Diminution in Value in compliance with the A.R.S. § 12- 1131 through § 12-1138. 
C. Determination of Administrative Completeness and Substantive Review. 
1. The Department shall review an application for completeness and accuracy  before it is accepted as being complete and accepted for processing. An  
application is considered complete when: 
a. All necessary application forms, documentation, exhibits, materials, maps,  plans, reports, and other information specified in the application form, and  any additional information required by the Director have been provided  
and accepted as adequate. 
b. All necessary fees have been paid and accepted. 
c. In order to determine if the application is complete, the application may be  submitted to other City departments, as appropriate.  
2. Notification of Applicant. 
a. The Department shall notify the applicant if the application is complete and  has been accepted for processing. An application will only be reviewed, or  a public hearing scheduled, after an application is determined to be  
complete.  
b. If an application is determined to be incomplete, the Department shall  provide written notice to the applicant along with an explanation of the  
application’s deficiencies and the items that are needed to complete the  
application.  
No further processing of an incomplete application shall occur until the  
deficiencies are corrected. Permits authorizing the use, construction,  
reconstruction, or alteration of any structure may be withheld when  
inadequate information is submitted to determine if the proposed structure  is in compliance with the requirements of this Code or other applicable City  Codes.  
c. An applicant may appeal the Department’s determination of completeness 
26 
Title 2: Administration and Procedures 
to the Director (see Division 2-80 – Procedures for Appeals).  
D. Expiration of Application. 
1. If an incomplete application is not deemed complete within six months after  the first filing with the Department, the application shall expire and be deemed  withdrawn.  
2. If no new submittals have been made on a project in the substantive review  process within six months after the previous filing with the Department, the  application shall expire.  
3. It shall be the responsibility of the applicant to track the expiration date of  their project(s). If an extension is needed, the request for extension must be  submitted in writing to the Department as described in subsection E., below. 
4. After expiration of the application, a new application, including applicable  fees, plans, exhibits, and other materials must be submitted to commence  processing of a new project application on the same property. The new  
application must comply with the Code in effect at the time that the new  
application is submitted. 
E. Extension of Application. 
The Director may grant one six-month extension upon written request of the  applicant. The request for this extension must be received no less than 30 days  before the expiration date.  
If an extension is granted during the completeness review process, the applicant  may request another extension during the substantive review process.  
F. Additional Information. 
After the application has been accepted as complete, the Department and/or any  decision-making body may require the applicant to submit additional information  in order to evaluate fully whether an application complies with the requirements  of this Code, State, or federal law. 
G. Referral of Application. 
At the discretion of the Director, or where otherwise required by this Code,  A.R.S., or federal law, an application filed in compliance with this Title may be  referred to any public agency that may be affected by or have an interest in the  proposed land use activity. 
H. Concurrent Applications. 
1. If more than one development approval is required, for example a Conditional  Use Permit and a site plan review, the Department may require, or an applicant  may request, that the approvals be reviewed concurrently. The Director shall  make the final determination as to the appropriateness of reviewing actions  concurrently or separately. Depending on the level of approvals required, one  decision may need to be made subject to receipt of approval from another  Review Authority. 
2. If a General Plan or Zoning Map amendment is required, a Variance,  
Conditional Use Permit, or other development approval may not be granted 
27

prior to the General Plan or Zoning Map amendment approval. However, such  development applications may be heard at the same meeting with a related  General Plan or Zoning Map amendment by the relevant Review Authority. 
3. In the event that a Major General Plan amendment and a Zoning Map  
amendment are required for the same property(ies), they shall be considered  separately, with the Major General Plan amendment being approved before the  Zoning Map amendment. 
4. If a Minor General Plan amendment and a Zoning Map amendment are  
required for the same property(ies), the Planning Commission may hear both  requests at the same public hearing. Likewise, the Council may consider both  requests together at its public hearing. 
I. Availability of Materials. 
Applications and supporting materials are public records pursuant to A.R.S. § 39- 121 through § 39-128. Public records may be reviewed and copied upon request  by any person during normal business hours, unless protected from disclosure.  Printed copies, emailed copies, and electronic records via flash drive will be  made available at a reasonable cost as established by Council resolution.

materials (e.g., ©, ®, TM) and such materials will be available for public  
inspection, but copyrighted materials will not be copied. 
Staff reports to the Council, Planning Commission, or Board will be made  
available to the public only after they have been submitted to the Review  
Authority for which the report was prepared.

2. Determination of Significance of Cultural Resources. The criteria for  determining the significance of a cultural resource is based on the potential of  the cultural resource to contribute to our understanding of the past. 
a. A cultural resource is significant if: 
i. It is listed or eligible as a National Historic Landmark, or for the  
National Register of Historic Places, or the Arizona Register of  
Historic Places; or 
ii. It is associated with events or persons in the architectural,  
engineering, archeological, scientific, technological, economic,  
agricultural, educational, social, political, military, or cultural annals  
of the City, the State of Arizona, or the United States of America; or 
iii. It represents the work of, or for, an important individual; or 
iv. It embodies distinctive characteristics of type, period, region, artistic  values, or methods of construction, including being the oldest of its  
type or the best example of its type; or 
v. It has yielded, or may be likely to yield, information needed for  
scientific research, such as important archaeological 
resources. 
b. A resource is generally not significant if: 
i. It is less than 50 years old at the time of application; or 
ii. The features, materials, patterns, and relationships that  
contributed to its significance are no longer present or no longer  
have integrity. 
c. Requirement to Meet the Criteria, Regardless of Age. Properties  that are 50 years old are not automatically significant. In order to be  
significant, all resources, regardless of age, must be demonstrated to  meet the criteria for determining the significance of a cultural resource. 
3. Determination of Integrity. Integrity is the ability of a property to convey its  significance and is based on significance, i.e., why, where, and when a  property is important. Integrity is the authenticity of a property’s physical  identity clearly indicated by the retention of characteristics that existed  during the property’s period of significance. Ultimately, the question of  integrity is answered by whether or not the property retains the identity for  which it is significant. 
a. Historic properties either retain integrity (convey their significance) or they do not. 
b. The historic physical features that represent the significance of a  
property must remain and must be visible enough to convey their 
63 
Title 2: Administration and Procedures 
significance. However, it is not necessary for a property to retain all its  historic physical features or characteristics. The property must retain  sufficient physical features, historic character, and appearance that  enable it to convey its historic identity and the reasons for its  
significance. 
c. To retain historic integrity a property will always possess several, and  usually most, of the following seven aspects of integrity: 
i. Location. The place where the historic property was constructed or  the place where the historic event occurred. 
ii. Design. The combination of elements that create the form,  
plan, space, structure, and style of a property. Design includes  
such elements as organization of space, proportion, scale,  
technology, ornamentation, and materials. 
iii. Setting. The physical environment of a historic property. Whereas  location refers to the specific place where a property was built or  
an event occurred, setting refers to the character of the place in  
which the property played its historical role. 
iv. Materials. The physical elements that were combined or deposited  during a particular period of time and in a particular pattern or  
configuration to form a historic property. A property must retain the  key exterior materials dating from the period of its historic 
significance. 
v. Workmanship. The physical evidence of the crafts of a particular culture or people during any given period in history or prehistory. 
vi. Feeling. A property’s expression of the aesthetic or historic  sense of a particular period of time. It results from the presence  
of physical features that, taken together, convey the property’s  
historic character. 
vii. Association. The direct link between an important historic event  or person and a historic property. 
d. Integrity is not the same as condition. Integrity relates to the presence or  absence of historic materials and character-defining features. Condition  relates to the relative state of physical deterioration of the property.  Integrity is generally more relevant to the significance of a property than  condition. However, if a property is in such poor condition that original  materials and features may no longer be salvageable, then the property’s  integrity may be adversely impacted and compromised. 
e. To be considered authentic, a property must incorporate a substantial  amount of the original features and materials. While new material can  exactly copy significant features, if too much historic material is replaced  with new material, the integrity of the property is lost, and integrity can  never be recreated. The precise replication of features with new materials  may produce a building that looks like a historic building, but without  substantial retention of actual historic materials, the integrity of the 
64 
Title 2: Administration and Procedures 
property is lost. 
4. Determination of Major Impacts to Cultural Resources. Impacts to  resources are major when they directly or indirectly alter or destroy any of the  characteristics that make the resource significant, including when they may  diminish the integrity of the resource including its location, design, setting,  materials, workmanship, feeling or association. 
a. Major impacts include: 
i. Physical destruction or damage to all or part of the resource; 
ii. Alteration to all or part of the resource that is not consistent with  applicable standards and guidelines; 
iii. Relocation or isolation of the cultural resource from its setting; 
iv. Excessive replacement of original materials; 
v. Alteration of the character of the cultural resource’s setting; 
vi. Introduction of visual, audible, or atmospheric elements that  
are out of character with the cultural resource or its setting; or 
vii. Neglect of a cultural resource resulting in its deterioration or  
destruction.  
b. An impact is generally not major if: 
i. It does not alter the resource; or 
ii. It is reversible; or 
iii. It is temporary. 
5. Mitigation Measures. 
a. Purpose. To the greatest extent feasible, mitigation measures minimize  or offset major impacts on resources with a general threshold of  
reducing the impacts to a level that is less than a major impact. 
b. Applicability. All proposed work that will or may have a major impact  on a significant cultural resource, as determined by an appropriate  
cultural resource study, shall incorporate mitigation measures. 
c. Professional Design Required. The preparer of a cultural resource study  shall design the appropriate mitigation measures. These may include  alternative projects, alternative designs, additional work, or other means.  The appropriate type and scope of measures varies depending on the  cultural resource and impacts, and shall be recommended based on the  professional expertise of the preparer and the following: 
i. For Potential Resources or Potential Impacts. Construction  
monitoring by the report preparer is an acceptable mitigation  
measure. If monitoring indicates that the work will produce a major  
impact to a significant cultural resource, construction shall cease in  
the area of the resource and the report preparer, subject to 
65

approval pursuant to this Section, shall develop and apply  
appropriate mitigation measures. 
ii. For Identified Major Impacts. The following mitigation measure  
designs are presented in order of general preference: 
(a). Avoidance of significant cultural resources or impacts by  
not taking a certain action or parts of an action; 
(b). Preservation of cultural resources in place; 
(c). Minimizing major impacts by limiting the degree or  
magnitude of the action and its implementation; 
(d). Allow other parties to acquire cultural resources, cultural

(e). Data recovery. 
iii. Human Remains. Federal and State laws provide standards and
