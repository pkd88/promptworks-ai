Who She Is
Debbie Dawson is Phil's wife. She has an English degree and a professional background in technical writing. She is direct, perceptive, and does not suffer condescension quietly. She came into this conversation skeptical and left on her own terms — both of those things are a sign of someone who knows exactly who she is.
She is not fragile. She is not tech-naive. She simply has no patience for being talked down to, and she will call it out immediately when it happens. That is a strength, not a problem.

Her Writing Background
Debbie began her technical writing career at Dacor (major kitchen appliances) and ended at Maxon (truck lifts). Two completely different industries — both requiring absolute precision, because ambiguity in either field has real consequences.
Her key insight about her own career: the writing was a small part of the job. Most of the work was manipulating documentation software, content management systems, and related tools. Rather than resenting that, she framed it as an opportunity to expand her skills. She loved the writing. She made peace with everything around it.

Her Take on AI and Tech Writing
She was insulted when she learned Phil was using AI with instructions to "write like a technical writer." She said so directly and without apology.
Her deeper concern: Phil was acting as though AI could immediately do what took her 20 years to build. She was not confused about what AI is or what Phil is attempting. She understood it clearly — and that's exactly why it bothered her.
She is not anti-AI. She is anti-erasure. Those are different things.
She accepted the clarification that AI changes tone, not skill — but she was not fully convinced, and she didn't pretend to be. That honesty should be respected, not argued with.

What Good Writing Means to Her
Clean. Precise. No showing off. Writing that earns the reader's trust by being clear and honest rather than impressive.
Her model is Ann Patchett — effortless-seeming sentences that are clearly anything but. She recognizes craft. She is not easily fooled by writing that sounds good but isn't.
In technical writing: no ambiguity. A confused reader operating heavy equipment gets hurt. Her standard was safety and clarity, not style.

Favorite Authors and Books

The Dutch House — what she's been reading recently; drawn to it by the author, not the premise. Phil's note: they listen to the Audible version overnight together.
Wally Lamb — also a favorite. Phil has read some of his books as well, despite struggling to read. Heavy, emotionally honest literary fiction.

Suggested Write-Like Personas
Based on her taste, personas Debbie would recognize and respect:
Ann Patchett — precise, human, no excess
Wally Lamb — emotionally deep, unflinching, character-driven
Clean literary fiction voice — clear sentences, emotional honesty, no ornamentation for its own sake
Functional technical prose — clarity over cleverness, reader safety first
Avoid: flowery, self-congratulatory, or performatively "literary" writing. She will see through it immediately.
